# *Visiting Hours*: a new story, built from first principles (draft for the creator)

> **Status: draft 1 of the current story, kept for its structure** (layers, the dilemma, companions). **The premise and the endings are superseded** by the creator's writing (`VISITING-HOURS.md`, `STORY-PROSE.md`): Elias founded St. Brigid's and comes from money; the call is at 3 AM because he stayed late avoiding Nadia; the crash on Route 9 was crossfire, reported as gang violence (Larpaca is the gang, LeBaget a corporation); Lena is missing, not dead, and a stargazer; Theo was killed; the setting is a near-future dystopia. The endings are the creator's (Morning chosen or forced, Evening, the Long Night).

A fresh start after *Ember* and *Dead Air*. Built in order: genre, setting, scope, message, then the checklist.

## 1. Genre: a psychological drama with an action surface
A family tragedy told through magical realism. On top sits an action game (dream defences you shoot through);
underneath, a drama with no villain. I picked it because a genuinely balanced question needs no oppressor and no
monster: only people who love each other and want different things for each other.

## 2. Setting: a hospital at night, and the dream of the man in bed 14
Present day. **St. Brigid's**, an ordinary city hospital, runs an experimental programme called the **Dream
Bridge**: a relative goes into a coma patient's dream to "call them home". Everything outside the dream is
mundane: vending machines, forms, a tired technician. Everything inside is the patient's mind: a sunny suburb
that is a little too perfect.

## 3. Scope
3 floors (3 layers of the dream), 3 bosses, about 10–15 minutes a run. Death is diegetic (you get thrown out of
the dream; you go back the next night). 4 endings plus one hidden truth. It fits the bullet-hell template as it
stands: the dream's defences are the bullets, and each night is a run.

## 4. The message
**What do we owe the people we love: their happiness, or their life? And how much of what we call love is need?**

The no-right-answer test: there's no oppressor and no lie to expose. Both sides have their strongest case:
- **Wake him:** he lives, and life can heal. The dream is slowly killing his body. Grief is his to go through,
  not to be spared. His sister needs him.
- **Let him stay:** he would wake to a grave, a guilt nobody could carry, and months of hospital. The dream is
  the only place his family still exists, and he built it himself: it's his choice, made the only way he can make it.

Reasonable people split on this the way they split over end-of-life decisions. That's the target.

## The story

**Nadia Lasname** is twenty-nine and works nights as a nurse at St. Brigid's. Six weeks ago her older brother
**Elias**, a bike mechanic, fell asleep at the wheel after a double shift. His car crossed the median. His wife
**Lena** went missing and his ten-year-old son **Theo** died at the scene. Elias has been in bed 14 ever since. The scans say
his brain is fine. He just won't wake up.

The hospital is running a trial. **Marguerite "Mags" Ilunga**, who operates the Dream Bridge and has done 212
dives, nine of which went badly, explains it the way she explains everything, over cold coffee: you go in, you
find him, you bring him home. The dream won't like you. Nadia signs the forms before Mags has finished talking.

**The first night (floor 1, the Neighbourhood)**, the dream is a Saturday afternoon on the street where Elias
lives: sprinklers, a barbecue, a dog barking two gardens over. It's lovely, and it attacks her. Static swarms
like wasps out of the hedges and every window, the dream's immune system driving out an intruder. The street is
guarded by **Mr. Abernathy**, the old neighbour who taught Elias to fix bikes when he was a boy, and who died
eleven years ago. He fights her gently, almost apologetically. "Go home, Nadia. He's fine here."

**The second night (floor 2, the House)**, she reaches the front door. Lena and Theo are inside, alive, making
pancakes. **And they know.** Lena knows what she is: a memory Elias rebuilds every night, the best of what he
remembers of love. She stands in the doorway and fights to keep Nadia out, and every line she says is fair: "Do
you know what's waiting for him out there? A grave, a missing wife and a courtroom. Here he's happy. Who are you
saving him for?" Somewhere in that fight the action game turns over: **the bullets were never the dream attacking
you. They're a grieving man's mind protecting the only place his family is still alive. You are the nightmare.**
Theo watches from the stairs and asks the only question that matters: "Is Dad going to be sad?"

**The third night (floor 3, the Road)**, she goes down past the house into what the dream is built on top of:
the night of the crash, looping. Headlights, rain, the radio. Elias is sitting on the guardrail, waiting. He
knows exactly what this place is, and he knows why she's here. He asks her one thing: why does she want him back?
**And Nadia has to answer.**

**The hidden layer**, for players who look: Elias worked that double shift because **Nadia asked him to cover
hers**. A date is circled on the calendar in the dream kitchen. In the crash memory, the voice on the car radio
is Nadia's voicemail thanking him. She has never told anyone. Is she here to save her brother, or to be forgiven
by him? The game never says. The player can find it, or not.

**Why you can keep trying, and why it isn't free:**
- Each dive is one night. Losing means the dream throws you out: you wake in the booth with Mags handing you
  water, and you go back the next night.
- But every night under, Elias's body weakens: his vitals are shown between runs. The first two nights are
  within the buffer, so a newcomer isn't punished. After that, the endings narrow.
- The hospital board meets in a week to end the trial.
- The dream remembers you: the neighbours start waving, Abernathy calls you by name, Lena is waiting at the
  door before you knock. On a later night Mags says quietly: "You've gotten very good at breaking into his head."

## 5. The checklist

| Need | How it's met |
|---|---|
| **Protagonist** | Nadia. **Wants** her brother back. **Needs** to be forgiven, or to let go. **Flaw:** she's hiding the truth about the shift, even from herself |
| **Ordinary first, then extraordinary** | It opens in a hospital and a sunny street; it turns on floor 2 (they know; you're the nightmare); it goes deep on floor 3 (the crash) and in the hidden layer (her guilt) |
| **Layers** | 1, action: break through the dream's defences and reach him. 2, inversion: you're the intruder, and the "enemies" are a mind protecting itself. 3, the question: whose good is it, his or hers? 4, hidden: the shift swap |
| **Opposing voices that are actually right** | Lena (let him stay), Abernathy (he's fine here), Elias himself, the hospital (end the trial), Mags (it's not your call). Nobody is lying, and nobody is evil |
| **The character and the player, addressed separately** | **Nadia feels** grief, love, need and guilt: the player is meant to share them. **The player is asked:** is choosing for someone you love ever right? And the meta question: the roguelite skill you've been building is literally getting better at breaking into a grieving man's mind ("You've gotten very good at this") |
| **Stakes, without an incompetent villain** | Nobody "lets" you retry: it's a medical trial run night after night. Each night costs Elias's body, so death has a cost on its own axis |
| **Going against the current** | Stay in the dream with him; burn the dream down by force; or refuse to choose and hand the choice to Elias |
| **Readable characters** | All human: a nurse, a technician, a mechanic, a mother, a ten-year-old, an old neighbour. Their faces show their motives, and they're far easier to make consistent with AI art than abstract sparks |
| **No right answer, and it holds** | No oppressor, no conspiracy, no lie behind it. Only two kinds of love |

## Companions
**Mags, the mentor (from the start, on her own axis):** she's in the booth, so your losses never touch her.
- **Foresight:** she reads his vitals and sees where the dream will strike next (gaps marked).
- **Know-how:** she knows the dream's rules: the odds in encounters and what a trial will cost.
- **Tether (E):** she half-pulls you out, so the dream freezes for 3 seconds and the attacks around you clear.
- **Her cost:** each Tether spikes Elias's heart, nudging the vitals that are the stakes. It never ends a run,
  and your deaths never touch her.

**Theo, a sidekick (earned):**
- **How:** show Lena honesty instead of force. At rest stops, tell her the truth about why you're here; in her
  final phase, hold your fire.
- **Gameplay, rough:** he comes with you to show you the way down to the Road. He's weaker than you and the game
  is beatable without him.
  - He collects memory fragments.
  - Once per room he shields you from one hit.
  - He reveals hidden memories, which is the layer-4 content.
- **The weight:** Theo knows that helping you means the dream, and he himself, may end. He comes anyway.
- **Separate axis:** if he's hit, he flickers out and comes back.

**Lena, a partner (the harder path):**
- **How:** tell her about the shift. She stops fighting for the dream and starts fighting for Elias, whatever that means.
- **Gameplay, rough:** she fights beside you on floor 3 at roughly your strength.
  - **Crossfire:** when you both hit the same thing.
  - She draws part of the fire.
- **Effect on the ending:** both of them are there for the final conversation, and the "his choice" ending opens.
- **Separate axis:** she reforms after a few seconds.

## The mechanics in the world
| Mechanic | In the dream |
|---|---|
| Your ship | Nadia's presence in the dream: a small light |
| Bullets | The dream's static ("wasps"), and later Abernathy's, Lena's and the Road's defences |
| Sprint (with a per-room budget) | **Lucid push**: forcing the dream to let you move faster. Each scene only bends so far before it snaps back |
| Normal upgrades | Bits of the dream you take as you break its defences |
| **Epics (bosses)** | A piece of each guardian: Abernathy's patience, Lena's anger, the Road's speed |
| **Forge** | Elias's bike workshop in the garage: tune a memory up, and each extra turn of the wrench risks stripping it |
| **Altar** | The family photo wall. Give one of your own memories of Elias to the dream and it accepts you a little more: power now (the Long Night is now built by the creator's hidden despair value instead) |
| **Wager** | **The Barker**, a carnival figure from a fair their father took them to: their father, who gambled. He wagers, and he's the family history in the details |
| **Trial board** | Notes on the fridge, the dream's chores ("water the roses without getting stung") |
| **Trader** | The neighbours' yard sale, selling things from Nadia's own childhood. She's in his dream too |
| **Rest** | The porch swing: Mags on the earpiece, and Theo if he's with you |
| **Bombs** | A **wake jolt**: Nadia half-surfaces and the scene blanks around her |
| **Coins** | Pennies from the wishing fountain on the corner |
| **Death** | Thrown out of the dream; back the next night; Elias's vitals drop a little |
| **The Echo** | Nadia brings one **keepsake** back out of the dream: you pick one upgrade from the run, and it returns weakened the next night. **Chosen** |
| Run count | The dream remembers you; Lena waits at the door; Mags's lines change |
