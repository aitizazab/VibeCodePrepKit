# Visiting Hours: the story in prose

## The night it happened

Elias Lasname is an emergency doctor, and the founder of St. Brigid's, the hospital where his younger sister Nadia
works as a nurse. On a bad Saturday shift he snapped at her and implied that she only got her job because she's his
sister. It was half true. The hiring panel had split two against two between Nadia and another nurse, Rafael Duarte,
and Elias cast the deciding vote. She heard the remark for what it was, and it cut deep. Both of them are proud.
He's proud of what he built, and she's proud of having made her way alone.

That night Elias was supposed to get a lift home from Nadia. Instead he stayed late at the hospital, putting off the
drive with her. *[The bible has him fill in a health-care proxy form at 2:47 AM, naming Nadia to decide for him. You
rejected this: there's no reason for him to do it on an ordinary night, and it reads like a thriller plot.]*

At 3:04 AM he called her. She was awake enough to play the voicemail. She heard him ask, "You still good for picking
me up?", heard him say "you're probably asleep", and dropped the phone. The rest of the message, which the player
hears in the opening and Nadia never does, is an apology for the remark, and a suggestion that maybe he should call
Lena.

Three minutes later, he did. Lena, his wife, drove out to get him with their son Theo asleep in the back seat. Lena
offered to drive home, and Elias insisted on taking the wheel. On Route 9 the car drove into a fight between a
corporation, LeBaget, and Larpaca, *[the bible calls Larpaca a second corporation; your note says a gang, and that it
doesn't matter at this game's scope]*. Theo was killed. Elias was critically hurt, and his right hand was crushed.
Lena was never found. The passenger door was standing open.

*[The bible's Night 4 reveal had the car stopped, and Lena getting out and walking toward the lights with her hand
raised, before the shooting started. You cut it entirely; that part of the night needs new material.]*

The next morning the news called it crossfire, and the headline called it "gang violence". Nobody was charged.

So two people each believe the night is their fault. Nadia believes it's hers: she heard him ask and went back to
sleep, so he called Lena, and Lena brought Theo. Elias believes it's his: he stayed late to avoid her, called his wife
and son out at three in the morning, and insisted on driving. Neither has told anyone.

## Six weeks later

Elias lies in bed 14 at St. Brigid's. He's been six weeks in a coma that nobody can explain: his scans are normal,
he's on no sedation, and there's no medical reason he shouldn't wake. He just doesn't.

Nadia works the ward around him. Rafael, the nurse who lost the job to her, now covers her extra hours. In a closed
sleep-research wing works Mags, a former doctor with an unlicensed rig, and Mags can send Nadia into her brother's
dream. Mags has done it before. In a curtained bed on that wing lies Julian Cho, a man who went into his wife's dream
eight months ago. She woke; he asked to stay, and Mags let him. "That's the last time I decided anything for anyone."

Inside, Elias's dream is one Saturday, repeating. It's the street they grew up on, at golden hour: sprinklers, a
barbecue, neighbours, and his family alive. He has lived it 41 times, once for every night since the crash.

The game's question is the question Nadia faces. Does she bring him back to a life where his son is dead, his wife is
missing, his hands are ruined and he believes he called them to their deaths? Or does she let him stay in the only
place his family still exists? Underneath it sits a harder one: is she trying to wake him for his sake, or so that she
can be forgiven?

## Night 1: The Street

The first day teaches the job. Nadia arrives for her shift, Rafael hands over ("He had a quiet one"), and she does her
rounds: medicines, wristbands, vitals. In room 14 she finds what came in with her brother. There's his chart, his
bag of effects with a keyring tagged "W.A. workshop" and a memorial card for a Walter Abernathy who died in 2015, a
family photo, and a bouquet from LeBaget signed by someone in "Community Partnerships". At the station is the police
report: the timeline, the open passenger door, Lena listed as missing, and the call log. That log shows a call to Nadia
at 3:04 that rang out, then a call to Lena at 3:07 that lasted two minutes. No one comments on it.

On her phone is the voicemail: Elias, 41 seconds, played to 0:09. If the player tries to play it, Nadia says "Not
now." She won't play it, all game, until an ending. A player who puts the call log next to the voicemail can work out
the worst of it on day one: he called Lena three minutes after her, because she didn't come. Nadia writes it in her
journal and scribbles it out.

At eleven she goes to the closed wing. Mags shows her the booth, the rig and the journal. Nadia asks about the
curtained bed, and Mags says "Don't." Then comes the first small ritual of the evening: her phone needs charging. Plug
it in? Nearly everyone says yes.

In the dream, Larch Street glows. Elias is at the barbecue, laughing, and always just too far away to reach. At the
bottom of the street is Walt's bike shop; at the top, their father's house, the gate chained and the curtains drawn.
In here Nadia moves the way Elias remembers her, like the fearless kid on the bike. The creek she jumped at seven
becomes her first double jump. She finds Walt alive and waving, and the day's newspaper. In Walt's repair ledger the
same line is written 41 times on the same date: "Sat. E., rear tyre, flat. Patched." Put together, they tell her two
things. Walt is long dead, so this is memory. And the dream is a loop.

Walt is the first argument, at his workbench, and he teaches her how to argue. When she gets too close to the truth,
the dream notices her. The lawns roll up behind her like carpet and the houses fold flat like a stage being struck,
and she runs for the end of the street until Mags pulls her out.

## Night 2: The House

*[The bible's day here starts the clock: the LeBaget representative visits with fruit he can't eat, announces the
transfer to Halcyon, and asks, gently, "Has he said anything?". See the note above.]*

Rafael, if she seeks him out, was on duty when they brought Elias in. Elias said one thing in the trauma bay: "Did Lena
get out?" Across town, if Nadia spends the time, is Lena's pottery studio *[your round 3 says Lena has no clay and no
workwear, so this needs replacing]*. A friend there says, carefully, that Lena hadn't been in much that last month,
though she'd told Elias she was working late on Thursdays. That night, if Nadia goes back to the curtained bed,
she reads Julian Cho's chart: a diver who went in and didn't come out. Mags finds her there: "I said don't."

In the dream the neighbours now wave and call "Morning, Nadia!", which is sweet, and wrong. Then comes the house:
their family home, at noon, with rain coming. Lena and Theo are inside making pancakes. The house won't let her in
straight. Hallways stretch, and doors lead back to the kitchen, and the stretched hallway is where she learns to run
along walls. Theo wants to play hide-and-seek, which is also the best way to find memories. His height marks on the
door frame stop at ten. The table is set for four, and the fourth chair is Nadia's, from the Sunday pancakes she
stopped coming to. On the fridge calendar every Thursday has a little key drawn on it, in Elias's hand. Dream-Lena
says, "Elias drew those. I never asked." She's his memory, so he noticed, and never asked either.

Theo asks her the question for the first time: "Is Dad going to be sad?" Whatever she answers, he remembers.

Lena knows what she is. She's a memory and she knows it, and she argues like it, at the kitchen table. In the middle
of the argument she tells Nadia what Nadia never let herself put together: "He called you first. You didn't pick up.
So he called me." If Nadia already worked it out on the first day, she can simply admit it: "I know." Lena respects
that. Theo's drawings slide across the table, and one of them is labelled "THE BAD DREAM": a woman in scrubs.

Nadia is the nightmare. The dream's defences aren't a monster's. They're a grieving mind protecting the only place
his family is alive, and to that mind she's the intruder. The house tips over as she runs. Floors become walls, the
staircase becomes a slide, and she escapes out the front door into the rain. From now on Lena watches from the window.

## Night 3: The Shift

On the third day Nadia reads the surgeon's note on his hand: crush injury, tendons repaired, a poor outlook for fine
motor work. She knows before the player does what that means for an emergency doctor. He won't intubate again. In his
office, if she looks, is the hiring file: the panel split two to two, his deciding vote, and six months later a new
senior post, paid from his own discretionary fund and given to Rafael. The remark was half true, and he knew it, and
he tried to even it up. With Lena's key, the empty family house holds Theo's room, Lena's coat with a Thursday parking
stub from across the river, and an unopened letter from the family trust's lawyers. They're seeds, nothing more.
*[The bible also puts the proxy form in his office here; you rejected it.]*

In the dream, past the house, where the map should end, is St. Brigid's as Elias dreams it. It's pastel and quiet, and
every patient smiles; every monitor draws a perfect line, and nobody dies on his shift. Nadia's own ID badge, swiped
at a ward door, flips the hospital between two moments. In one, the dream's shift: empty corridors, locked doors. In
the other, the shift as it really was: crowded, loud, gurneys everywhere. Behind one staff-room door, in the real
moment, plays the remark, as Elias remembers it: what he said, and what he saw on her face. She can watch it or walk
away. In her dream locker is a letter that says "Appointed on merit."

The guardian is the Doctor: Elias as everyone saw him on shift, sharp, impatient, and right about medicine. He argues
like a clinician: you don't bring a patient back to a life he wouldn't want. His last argument is the remark itself,
aimed at her right to decide anything for him. The honest answer admits the half truth. Then the monitors flatline
one after another down the corridor. Someone is finally dying in his hospital, and it's him. She escapes down gurney
slides and up a lift shaft. Under the hospital is the road. Mags, at the monitor afterwards: "One more night is safe.
Two isn't."

## Night 4: The Road

His vitals drop. If she goes to Route 9, the cordon is still up six weeks later. Against the barrier is a shrine of
notes from his patients ("You're the reason I'm alive, Doc"), with one wreath from LeBaget and none from Larpaca. If
Nadia has earned Mags's trust, Mags tells her about Julian Cho.

The dive goes straight down: the street dark for the first time, the house's windows lit, the hospital's basement,
then Route 9 at 3:48 AM, raining, the night looping. The war is weather here: two colours of searchlight, tracer fire
drifting down like slow paper streamers, corporate billboards along the road. If Theo trusts her, because she answered
him honestly, he's waiting at the top of the road with a torch: "I know the shortcuts." He runs ahead and lights the
way. Headlights come down the road behind her and she runs ahead of them.

Mid-chase, her phone rings: "Elias." If she answers, his voice asks, "You still good for picking me up?", and if she
says "On my way", the dream rewrites the night for her. She's driving, the rain eases, everyone's safe. It's beautiful,
and it skips the truth.

At the guardrail, Theo asks his question a second time: "If he wakes up, he'll know I'm gone. Is Dad going to be sad?"
Only an honest answer counts. If she says yes, he gives her the line she'll need later: "Tell him it's okay to be sad."
At the end of the road Elias sits on the guardrail beside the car. He looks up. "Nads?" He knows she's there. Mags
pulls her out: "Not tonight. His heart."

## Night 5: The Car

*[You noted the car is worth considering, but only if there's a reason they end up back at the beginning.]*

On the last day nothing is required. Nadia can say what she wants to Rafael, to Mags, to the others, and sit in room
14. The voicemail is still "Not now."

The dream doesn't fight her any more. The street is empty, the house dark, the hospital silent, the rain soft. The car
is parked on the shoulder of Route 9 with the engine off and the dashboard clock at 3:48. Elias is in the driver's
seat. Nadia opens the passenger door and gets in. She's finally picking him up.

The last conversation uses the argument's tools, but he isn't fighting her, and he says only: "Let me stay." Four
things hold him there:
- **Guilt**, because he called them out and he drove. It's released only by Nadia's confession that she heard him ask
  and went back to sleep.
- **Theo**: if he goes, he leaves him. It's released by Theo's blessing.
- **Lena**: in here she exists, and out there she's nowhere. It's released by what Lena gave Nadia, by Lena being
  there, or by a hope Nadia can't back up.
- **Purpose**: what is he without his hands? It's released by the Doctor's truth, by the patients she cared for, or
  by the shrine.

How she talks to him decides the ending.

## The endings

**Morning, chosen.** She tells him the truth, one piece at a time, and he tells her his side. They find out they've
been carrying the same night. Then she asks him what he wants. A long beat on the dashboard clock, then it moves to
3:49. "...Drive me home, Nads?" At the road, Theo asks him, "Are you going to be sad?", and Elias says "Yes. For a long
time." Theo says "Okay." The two of them run up through the layers together, and it isn't a chase. The dream lets
them go, each layer struck gently behind them like a stage set. Elias whoops on a wall jump. Walt waves, the
neighbours line the street, and the music plays in full. In the booth, Mags says very quietly, "His eyes are open."
His first words: "Where's Lena?" Nadia: "We don't know." Months later there's a grave for Theo, a bench with Lena's
name and no dates, and Elias with his hand in a brace, teaching residents. A missing poster hangs on the staff board,
and a LeBaget plaque in the lobby. In the last scene Nadia's phone is on her nightstand, charging, and it rings:
"Elias." She answers on the first ring. "You still good for picking me up?" "I'm already outside." The old voicemail
is marked played to the end; she listened with him, off screen. In the car he told her the remark wasn't true. She said
it was half true. "Rafael was good." "He was." They laughed.

**Morning, forced.** She tells him, "You're coming back. I'm not asking." Each push cracks the windscreen further. He
doesn't fight; he only says "Nads, don't." The dream comes apart and she drags him up through every layer by the
hand. He's heavy and he stumbles, and she can't double jump while holding him. The guardians remember how she treated
them: Walt holds the street open for her, or slams the garage door. The sky tears, and Mags says "Now." He wakes
gasping into the grief as if it were new: "Where's Theo? ...Where are they?" He recovers. He's courteous to her and
far away, and he sleeps with the lights on. She still drives him. Once, to the window, he says, "I had the strangest
dream. You were in it... I wish you'd let me finish it." Alone in the car afterwards, she finally plays the voicemail
and hears the apology, from a man who is alive and won't talk about that night. She carries two nights now.

**Evening.** She doesn't argue. She winds the window down, and the rain stops. The clock moves past 3:48 for the first
time, the light turns to dusk, and the car is parked on Larch Street with the porch lights coming on. The endless
Saturday finally gets its evening. She walks him up the street, and her double jump does nothing any more; there's
nowhere to run. Walt pulls his garage door down: "Evening, Nads." Lena and Theo are at the door. Elias goes in. Nadia
doesn't. She sits on the steps until the lights go out one by one. Over nine days his heart slows. On the last night,
at his bedside, she plays the voicemail all the way through for the first time and hears him say the remark wasn't
true. His heartbeat slows like someone falling asleep after a good day. Afterwards there are two graves and an empty
stone, and Nadia is back on shift.

**The Long Night.** Throughout the game, some choices are comfort: the porch swing, the lemonade, the fourth chair,
the "Appointed on merit" letter. If Nadia keeps choosing them, a quiet slope builds. *[The bible locks the route at
the phone ritual, "Your phone is at 3%. Leave it?"; you want to rethink it as a strange, curious choice that sneaks up
on the player.]* After the lock, the waking world starts to flatten into the dream's billboards: Rafael is a cut-out,
a corridor is a painted flat, and the board in room 14 reads "Saturday". Only Mags stays solid. On the last night the
dream welcomes her: Walt has fixed her old bike, and Lena has set the fourth plate. In the car Elias says "Stay with
me", and every answer she has left is yes. Morning comes for her, white light and Mags calling her name, and she runs
away from it, toward the house. It never catches her, even if she stops, and that's what makes it wrong. At 3 AM in the
dream her phone rings. She answers on the first ring, "On my way", and picks him up, and they drive home safely, and
the clock resets to Saturday morning. In the booth, Mags sits alone with three monitors now: Julian Cho, room 14 and
the chair. She plugs in Nadia's dead phone. It lights: one voicemail from Elias, played to 0:09. She never heard the
apology.

## What the game never answers

There are three threads the game only hints at:
- **Where Lena is.** She'd been lying about her Thursdays, the passenger door was open, and Elias's first question
  was whether she got out.
- **The crossfire.** Who was fighting on Route 9, and why LeBaget sends flowers and wants Elias moved.
- **The family money.** *[Your note: the Lasnames were immigrants, and it's unclear whether Elias inherited the
  fortune as the eldest son or earned it. Nadia refuses to use his money while he's in a coma. Their parents are
  outside this game's scope.]*

Each thread gets seeds in both worlds. No character ever explains one, and no ending resolves it.
