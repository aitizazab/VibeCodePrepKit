A ten-year-old boy's crayon drawing on a sheet of paper, filling the frame, seen straight on: his nightmare, inside their house at night. In the front on the left, the mum and the boy sit at the kitchen table with plates of pancakes in front of them and one empty chair pulled out beside them, their crayon mouths turned down, both looking across at the dad. On the right, at the back of the room, the front door stands wide open onto the dark night, and the dad is walking out through it: drawn small and far away, turned away from them with his back to us, mid-step over the doorstep, in green surgical scrubs and a green surgical cap, a little black pager on his waistband, a long dark crayon shadow stretching behind him across the floor toward the table. Through the open door and the kitchen window, a dark night sky scribbled hard in black and purple crayon, the scribbles pressed so hard they shine, a small sad moon. Drawn like a real child's drawing: wobbly, uneven, frightened, the colours going over the lines.

Across the top, in the boy's own wobbly hand-printed capital letters in red crayon: THE BAD DREAM. Those three words are the only writing in the picture; no other text, no numbers, no signature, no watermark.
