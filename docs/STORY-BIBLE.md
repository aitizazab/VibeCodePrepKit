## 0. The spine on one page
**What happened:** a man stayed late to avoid his sister, called her at 3 AM, and she heard him ask and fell asleep.
So he called his wife, who brought their sleeping son, and on the way home a fight between LeBaget, a corporation, and Larpaca, a gang (// corpo and a gang, but it doesn't matter within the scope of this game) went through their car. Theo was killed; Lena is missing; Elias is in a coma. The people who fired the guns face nothing.

**The question for the player:** what do we owe the people we love, their happiness or their life? And how much of
what we call love is need? (Draft 1's message, unchanged.) Underneath it is what Nadia is actually guilty of: survivor's
guilt (the creator's framing; to be worked out together, because it's at the heart of the endings).

**Both sides at their strongest (the no-right-answer test):**

| Wake him | Let him stay |
|---|---|
| He's alive, and grief can be lived through. It's his to go through, not to be spared | He'd wake to his son's grave, a wife missing with no answers, a ruined right hand that ends his medicine, and the belief that he called them out that night |
| His body is failing under the dream, and every night costs his heart | The dream is the only place his family still exists, and he built it himself: it's his choice, made the only way he can make it |
| His sister needs him, and so do his patients | "Needing him" is exactly the selfishness he'd see through |

No oppressor sits inside the dilemma. The corporation and the gang are the cause and the background; they never become
the answer to the question.

**The two guilts (the emotional engine):**
- **Nadia's: survivor's guilt**, not blame for the crash (she isn't responsible for gang violence). She believes herself
  at least partly responsible for what happened to Lena and Theo, and that the world would have been better off with
  Lena in it than her. What Lena did or was that makes that make sense: undecided.
- **Elias's:** he stayed late to avoid her, then called his wife and son out at 3 AM. Who drove doesn't matter. What he
  actually feels guilty about, and whether he comes to it over the nights or represses what he already remembers:
  undecided.

In the Morning endings Nadia believes Lena wouldn't want either of them to succumb to their darkness (section 10).

## 3. Night by night

### Night 1: The Street
**The day (Day 1, six weeks after; the tutorial day).**
- Nadia arrives for her shift. **Rafael** hands over the night ("He had a quiet one."). He's been covering her extra
  hours as an intern. The duty loop is taught here: one meds round, read the wristbands, chart the vitals.
- **Room 14:** the chart (`fact_chart`: no reason he shouldn't wake), the effects bag (`fact_effects`: a keyring tagged
  "W.A. workshop", a memorial card "Walter Abernathy, 1941–2015"), the family photo (`fact_photo`), and a bouquet with
  a LeBaget card signed by someone in "Community Partnerships".
- **The police envelope** at the station (`fact_police_report`): the timeline, "passenger door found open", "L. Lasname:
  missing", and the call log (3:04 to N. Lasname, rang out; 3:07 to L. Lasname, 2 min). **Nobody comments on it.**
- **Her phone** (`fact_voicemail`): the voicemail from Elias, marked as played (it auto-played to its end while she
  fell asleep). Selecting it: "Not now." The player knows what's on it. When she hears the rest, and what it does to
  her: undecided.
- **The optional deduction** (the bomb, for players who look): the call log plus the voicemail's first line gives
  `ded_why_lena_came`: *"He called Lena three minutes after me. Because I didn't come."* Nadia scribbles it out
  (legible if you look closely).
- **11 PM, the closed sleep-research wing.** Mags, the booth, the rig, the journal. Mags's briefing is the creator's
  scene (`STORY-PROSE.md`): Dream Walking is hard; she won't follow Nadia in; "Five Nights, maybe Six before it's too
  dangerous"; inside, only the Truth is on her side; stay calm, or Elias could be harmed. The first phone ritual: "Plug
  it in?" (innocuous; most will say yes).

**The dive.** Larch Street on a Saturday at golden hour: lawns, sprinklers, a barbecue, Biscuit the dog, Mrs. Lindqvist
at her roses, Mr. Okafor at the grill. **It's the street the Lasname kids grew up on**: Walt's bike shop at the bottom,
their father's house on the hill at the top, gate chained, curtains drawn. Elias is at the barbecue, laughing, always
just too far away to reach. The creator wants this space dense enough for a later stealth section (Dishonored's
Distillery district as the reference); whether one golden-hour street can be that dense, or links to a denser place, is
undecided.
- Movement wakes up ("You're lighter in here." Mags): the creek Nadia jumped on her bike at seven becomes the first
  double jump. In the dream she moves the way **he remembers her**: the fearless kid on the bike.
- Memories: Walt waving (`mem_walt_alive`), Saturday's newspaper (`mem_newspaper`), "Abernathy Cycles, since 1971"
  (`mem_shop_sign`), and **Walt's repair ledger** (`mem_ledger`): "Sat. E., rear tyre, flat. Patched." written 41 times
  on the same date.
- Deductions: `ded_walt_dead` (the card + Walt alive); `ded_same_saturday` (the paper + the ledger: "He's lived this
  Saturday 41 times. One for every night since the crash.").
- **Battle: Walt**, at his workbench. The creator rejected the tutorial as currently built; what teaches the argument,
  and whether Walt's battle survives in another form, is undecided (after the setting talk).
- **The exit:** the dream notices her. The stage-strike escape is rejected with the tutorial; how Night 1 ends is
  undecided. Mags pulls her up.

**What's learned:** Walt is a memory; the dream is a loop; Elias is there and keeps his distance.
**What changes:** the dream knows a stranger is inside. Tomorrow the neighbours will know her name.
**Spectrum:** the first Force choice is small and sensible (press Walt, or force the garage lock instead of finding
the key). Which choices feed the despair value is undecided (section 7).

### Night 2: The House
**The day (Day 2).**
- **An unwelcome visitor** comes to room 14. It can be LeBaget (the creator: "It can be LeBaget, but I'm open to other
  ideas").
- **Rafael** (optional, staff room): he was on when they brought Elias in. Elias said one thing in the trauma bay:
  **"Did Lena get out?"** (`fact_did_lena_get_out`).
- **Optional:** something that shows a hole in who Lena is (in place of the old studio friend; undecided). She'd told
  Elias she was working late on Thursdays; what she was really doing is undecided.
- The duty trade-off begins in earnest (see `NIGHTS-GAMEPLAY.md`): every trip off the ward is a round Rafael covers.

**The dive.** The street again, shorter: the neighbours now wave and say "Morning, Nadia!" (cute, and wrong). Then
**the house**: the Lasname family home rebuilt, noon, rain coming. Lena and Theo inside, making pancakes. The house
won't let her in straight: hallways stretch, a door leads back into the kitchen. **Wallrun** arrives on the stretched
hallway ("Walls in there are only as solid as he remembers them."). The house playing with her is the movement intro.
- **Lena keeps Elias away from Nadia**: she fears Nadia would convince him to leave the place where he's happy. Nadia
  sits down for pancakes and plays hide-and-seek, accepting the excuse to spend time with the family she lost, thinking
  Elias will be there soon to join them.
- **Theo** wants to play hide-and-seek: the stealth intro, and the best way to find the memories scattered around the
  house. The player needs to know how many are left, or at least when none are: a line like *"I'm not sure if there's
  anything left to find. I could let Theo find me here..."*, ending differently on the Despair route and the normal
  route ("but what does it matter, when he's not even real?" or "it would be nice to see his smile again"; which goes
  with which route is undecided). His height marks on the kitchen doorframe stop at 10.
- **The table is set for four.** The fourth chair is Nadia's.
- **The calendar** on the fridge: "Pancakes!" on every Sunday, and a small star on every one of Lena's Thursdays (her
  stargazing; 2026-10-12).
- Theo asks the question for the first time: **"Is Dad going to be sad?"** The answer is remembered (honest, comfort,
  or silence). An honest answer is what later brings Theo to her side.
- Deductions: `ded_she_knows` (Lena knows what she is); `ded_saturday_off` (in the dream Elias is home on Saturday,
  but the real Elias worked that day: "This isn't a memory. It's the Saturday he didn't have.").
- **Battle: Lena**, at the kitchen table. Elias never comes. Nadia questions Lena, and Lena admits she isn't willing to
  let him go. Alongside an option that pacifies her, there's one that antagonises her further (arguing she's in the
  wrong for acting like she's real when she isn't, avoiding her question); that way she condemns Nadia. The battle ends
  with Lena unsure of her conviction (amenable to giving her blessing in later battles) or condemning Nadia. "He called
  you first" stays in the argument (the creator rewrote the battle script); this argument is what lets Lena change her
  mind later, if Nadia plays her cards right.
- **Escape:** either way, the house kicks Nadia out. It tips: floors become walls, the staircase becomes a slide, the
  hallway turns over while she runs along it. Out the front door, into the rain. A closed umbrella sits by the exit;
  picked up with the dream tool she's learnt, it lets her stand in the rain and carry on normally. Without it she can
  carry on anyway, which keeps the Despair route open (if it isn't already aborted). Lena condemning her makes the
  Despair route easier to get onto.

**What's learned:** the dream's people know what they are. Elias unconsciously wants her not to disrupt his few
moments of happiness, and Lena keeps him from her. **She is the intruder.**
**What changes:** the dream defends itself by name. Lena watches from the window from now on. Mags's lines get
quieter.

### Night 3: The Shift
**The day (Day 3).**
- **The hand:** the surgeon's note in room 14 (`fact_hand`): crush injury, tendon repair, poor prognosis for fine
  motor function. Nadia knows what that means for an ER doctor before the player does: "He won't intubate again."
- **Optional:** his office and the hiring file (`fact_hiring_file`): the panel's split and the founder's casting vote.
- **Optional:** the empty Lasname house, sealed and dusty (how she gets in is undecided). Theo's room. Lena's coat with
  a parking stub from a district across the river, dated a Thursday. Whether a letter from the family trust stays is
  undecided (it only matters if clues about the money stay in). Seeds only.
- **Optional:** if Nadia has neglected duties, Rafael confronts her at the nurses' station (a short waking-world
  argument; no pressure phase, because the waking world has none).

**The dive.** The street is shuttered now (the dream has gone defensive); Lena watches from the window. Past the
house, where the dream's map should end, is **St. Brigid's as Elias dreams it**: pastel, quiet, every bed holding a
smiling patient. In his hospital nobody dies on his shift.
- **The two moments:** Nadia's own ID badge, swiped at a ward door, flips the hospital between **the dream's shift**
  (empty corridors, locked doors) and **the shift as it was** (crowded, loud, doors open, gurneys everywhere). Same
  walls, two moments (Titanfall 2's *Effect and Cause* idea, with a badge instead of a time device). The creator
  questioned what the contrast is for: keep, change or drop is undecided.
- In "as it was", a staff-room door plays **the remark**, as Elias remembers it: what he said, and what he saw on her
  face. Watching it is optional.
- The "Appointed on merit" letter in her dream locker and the "perfect line" monitors were questioned by the creator
  (the letter reads as mocking; a straight line on a monitor is a flatline): keep, fix or cut is undecided.
- **Battle: the Doctor**, in the trauma bay, at the lightbox. Elias as he was on shift: sharp, impatient, right about
  medicine. His claim is a clinician's: "You don't bring a patient back to a life he wouldn't want." Its last premise
  is the remark itself, aimed at her right to decide; the honest answer admits the half truth. What else he argues,
  what wins it now that the proxy form is cut, and what the Doctor's truth is: undecided. The creator: possibly the
  most important battle, the player's first introduction to Elias's character.
- **Escape: code blue.** The monitors flatline one after another down the corridor (in his hospital someone is
  finally dying: him). Gurney slides, a lift-shaft chimney climb, the badge flipping the corridor mid-run.

**What's learned:** the dream is the Saturday he should have had (no shift, no remark).
**What changes:** the hospital's floor gives way; the road is underneath. Mags, at the monitor afterwards: "One
more night is safe. Two isn't."

### Night 4: The Road
**The day (Day 4; short and tense).**
- Elias's vitals have dropped.
- **Optional:** Route 9. Six weeks on, the cordon is still up. A shrine of notes and flowers from St. Brigid's patients
  against the barrier (`fact_shrine`: "You're the reason I'm alive, Doc"). One wreath from LeBaget; none from Larpaca.
- **Mags** may tell Nadia something if Nadia has earned her trust (what earns it is undecided). Julian Cho is cut, with
  no replacement.

**The dive.** Straight down: the street dark for the first time, the house's windows lit, the hospital's basement, and
then **Route 9 at 3:48 AM**, raining, looping. The war is dream-weather: two colours of searchlight sweeping the road,
tracer fire falling like slow paper streamers, billboards lining the road.
- **Theo** (if earned on Night 2) waits at the top of the road with a torch: "I know
  the shortcuts." He runs ahead and lights the route: the game's "follow the light", made a child. Without him the
  route is in low light or none, lit only by the sweep of the headlights (an escape without a timer: "Seems cool", the
  creator).
- **The chase:** the car's headlights come down the road behind her, looping, and she runs ahead of them: over cars,
  along billboards, under barriers. Three legs with two breathers.
- **The phone rings in her pocket** in the middle of the chase: "Elias". Answering slows the world, and his voice says
  "You still good for picking me up?" If she says "On my way", the dream rewrites the night for her: she's driving,
  the rain eases, everyone's safe. It's beautiful, and it skips the truth. What skipping it costs is undecided. (In the
  Long Night route both options say "Answer".) Letting it ring keeps her running.
- **The truth**, at the crash point: undecided. Lena walking into the light is cut. What the memory shows, what leads
  to it, and why it matters are open.
- **Theo's question, the second time**, at the guardrail: "If he wakes up, he'll know I'm gone. Is Dad going to be
  sad?" A conversation with one rule: only honest answers count (this teaches the **Tell** verb). An honest answer gives **Theo's blessing** (`truth_theo`: "Tell him it's okay to be sad.").
- **Force route:** Lena breaks the rules here (section 7).
- At the end of the road, Elias sits on the guardrail beside the car. He looks up: **"Nads?"** Mags pulls her out:
  "Not tonight. His heart."

**What's learned:** the Night 4 truth (undecided); Theo's blessing; that Elias, deep down, knows she's here.
**What changes:** the final conversation is set.

### Night 5: The Car
**The day (Day 5; short; everything optional).** Nadia can say what she wants to say to Rafael, Mags or the others,
chart her brother one last time, and sit in room 14. The voicemail is still "Not now." In the Long Night route the day
is wrong (section 7).

**The dive.** The dream doesn't fight her any more: the street is empty and dim, the house is dark, the hospital
silent. The road is quiet; the rain is soft. The car is parked on the shoulder, engine off, the dashboard clock at
3:48. Elias is in the driver's seat. **Nadia opens the passenger door and gets in.** She's picking him up. (The car as
the final arena is undecided: only if there's a reason they end up back at the beginning.)

The final talk uses the battle's verbs; he isn't fighting her. His claim is "Let me stay." The creator's idea: Lena and
Theo might appear in the back seat to support Nadia's arguments, instead of Nadia just passing on their wishes. What
decides the ending: section 9. (In the Long Night route the last dive goes differently: section 10.)

## 5. The layers
1. **Action (everyone gets this):** break into a dream, outrun it, win arguments, bring your brother home. A complete
   story on its own.
2. **Inversion (Night 2):** you're the intruder. The defences are a mind protecting the only place his family is
   alive, and his wife is keeping him from you.
3. **The question (Night 3):** is it her call? What would he want? Whose good is she serving? And what is she really
   guilty of?
4. **Hidden (for players who look):** she heard him ask; the tie-break; Lena's Thursdays; the family money; the two
   framings of the news; LeBaget's part, if it had one. None of it is resolved.
5. **Aimed at the player:** the game spends four nights teaching you to **win** arguments and to get very good at
   breaking into a grieving man's mind. Its best ending asks you to stop winning and **tell**. Mags notices, on
   Night 4: "You've gotten very good at this."

## 10. The endings in full
The creator's endings (written 2026-10-11 in `STORY-PROSE.md`) replace this bible's earlier ones. What they don't
cover yet is undecided.

### How each is reached
| Ending | How it's reached | Locked out by |
|---|---|---|
| **Morning, chosen** | She convinces him (section 9) | the Long Night lock-in |
| **Morning, forced** | He won't see reason, and she's willing to force him out | the lock-in |
| **Evening** | She fails to convince him, or chooses to let it rest | the lock-in |
| **The Long Night** | The Despair route (the lock-in rule is undecided) | aborting the route (how: undecided) |

### Morning (chosen and forced)
Nadia believes Lena wouldn't want either of them to succumb to their darkness.
- **Chosen:** she convinces Elias of that. When it's amicable, Nadia takes Elias out of the city to go stargazing, to
  show him that Lena lives on in his heart.
- **Forced:** she's willing to force him out if he won't see reason.

The stargazing is Morning, chosen's last image (the creator). Both need a complete redo, to be talked through with the
creator. Everything else is undecided: the way out of the dream, his waking, his first words and the epilogue.

### Evening
She fails to convince him that Lena wouldn't want them to give in to the dark, or she chooses not to argue and lets it
rest. Elias stays, and dies in his sleep; Nadia
has another dead relation to carry her guilt for. It ends with a memorial and a funeral, Nadia left on her own as it
rains.

The creator liked the earlier Evening from the point where Elias goes inside, so those beats are the starting point:
on Larch Street at dusk, Walt pulls his garage door down ("Evening, Nads."), Lena and Theo are at the door, Elias goes
in, and Nadia doesn't. She sits on the steps until the lights inside go out, one by one. Over the following days his
heart slows like someone falling asleep after a good day. (Whether she plays the voicemail at his bedside here depends
on when she hears the rest of it: undecided.)

### The Long Night
1. **The slide.** Nadia starts with conviction that Lena and Theo wouldn't blame her, but her bad habits and unhealthy
   schedule leave her vulnerable to the manifestations of guilt in her mind, until she reaches the point of no return
   and starts thinking she's unredeemable.
2. **The warning.** Her fragile state of mind destabilises the dreamscape. Mags warns her, scolding her for even
   thinking of starting a dive in such a state; Nadia doesn't hear her out.
3. **The battle.** In the dream she tries to convince Elias as before, but he doesn't even let her finish her argument,
   seeing her lack of conviction. The battle is a scripted loss: technically beatable, near impossible, so it feels less
   scripted than it is.
4. **The hand.** Once Nadia is defeated, Elias asks her to stay. None of the dialogue gets her off the path, which ends
   with her taking his hand. Mags screams at her not to: her connection to the real world will be lost if she accepts
   her place in the dream. Nadia ignores her. Mags's last words tell her that the Elias in front of her isn't real: he
   already died near the start of the battle, from the stress her unstable state caused the dream. Nadia doesn't hear
   it. She sees only the offer to live happily with her true family for however long she has left.
5. **The last image:** undecided (the creator asked for suggestions). The default is a shot of Nadia's exhausted face,
   letting go after carrying her burden for so long.

## 11. The creator's question: is "His Choice" shown?
**Yes.** Morning absorbs His Choice: if she convinces him, Morning (chosen) plays; if she fails, Evening. It's his
decision, so it can go against her, and it's granular: everything she brought him counts, and he names the big
things. The journal shows her what she has before the final talk, so the outcome is earned, not random.
